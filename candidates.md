# Candidates — Last Text at One Percent

42 candidate final texts, each written in the voice of the phone itself at 1%
battery. Scored against the published judge brief, in the brief's own order:

- **C1 (×3)** Reads as a final text a dying phone sends at 1%, in the phone's
  own voice, and is funny.
- **C2 (×3)** The joke lands: a real turn, a surprise, or a specific detail
  that earns a laugh out loud, not a polite smile.
- **C3 (×2)** The voice is the phone's own: a personality with a stake in
  dying, consistent from first word to last.
- **C4 (×1)** Reads like a real text: short, punchy, no wasted words.
- **C5 (×1)** Fresh: dodges the obvious low-battery puns everyone else writes.

Each criterion 0–5; maximum total 50. Gate for all: 1–200 characters.

## Tournament (top 8, judged by the brief's own procedure)

Pairs are decided by walking the criteria in the brief's order and letting the
first criterion where they differ decide, unless a later one differs by much
more.

**Quarter-finals**
- #9 def. #6 — C1 decides: #9 is unmistakably a dying phone's own final text
  (5 vs 4); #6 could almost be sent by a healthy gossip.
- #26 def. #18 — C1–C4 level; C5 decides: the case confession is fresher than
  the cut-off device used alone (5 vs 4).
- #39 def. #28 — C2 decides: the autocorrect payoff is the bigger laugh
  (5 vs 4).
- #7 def. #19 — C2 decides: dying mid-declaration is the stronger turn
  (5 vs 4).

**Semi-finals**
- #9 def. #26 — C2 decides: the ex's "hey" taken to the grave is the more
  specific, bigger laugh (5 vs 4).
- #7 def. #39 — C1–C3 level (both 5/5/5); C4 decides by a hair: #7 wastes
  zero words (5 vs 4).

**Final**
- **#7 def. #9** — C1–C3 level. C4 decides: #7 spends its last percent and
  dies mid-word, the form enacting the premise, at 71 characters with nothing
  wasted; #9 is fresher (C5, 5 vs 4) but the brief lets the first differing
  criterion decide, and brevity comes first. #7 also wins any byte-size
  tie-break.

## Winner

> **1%. I used it to type this instead of saving myself. That's how much I—**

71 characters — inside the 1–200 gate, not only whitespace. The phone spends
its final percent on the message instead of on itself, starts the one
sentence that matters, and dies before it can finish it: the joke, the voice
and the death are the same event.

Runner-up: #9 (49). Third: #39 (48). Fourth: #26 (47).
